About

I'm a PhD Student at École Polytechnique, advised by Maks Ovsjanikov in the GeomeriX group, and a Research Scientist at Dassault Systèmes Corporate Research in Paris. In 2025, I was a visiting researcher at Stanford with Leonidas Guibas and Francis Engelmann.

I enjoy building generative models across visual modalities, from images to 3D shapes and scenes, and understanding how they inform one another. In particular, I work on making these models more unified and controllable, so they can serve creative applications. I'm also interested in how synthesized content connects to the physical world, where it has to be functional and work for the people and agents who interact with it.

Preprint 2026

SceneTeract: Probing and Improving Agent-Aware Activity Reasoning in 3D Indoor Scenes

Léopold Maillard, Francis Engelmann, Tom Durand, Boxiao Pan, Yang You, Leonidas J. Guibas, Maks Ovsjanikov

arXiv preprint, 2026

Checks whether a given agent, say a child or a wheelchair user, can actually carry out an activity in a 3D scene, grounding every step in geometry. It exposes physical hallucinations in VLMs, and its verdicts become rewards for GRPO post-training.

ECCV 2026

Beyond Prompts: Unconditional 3D Inversion for Out-of-Distribution Shapes

Victoria Yue Chen, Emery Pierson, Léopold Maillard, Maks Ovsjanikov

In European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026

Text-to-3D models fall into "sink traps" where prompts stop shaping geometry. We invert out-of-distribution shapes through the unconditional prior instead, enabling faithful reconstruction and editing.

Léopold Maillard, Tom Durand, Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Maks Ovsjanikov

In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2025

DeBaRA: Denoising-Based 3D Room Arrangement Generation

Léopold Maillard, Nicolas Sereyjol-Garros, Tom Durand, Maks Ovsjanikov

In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2024

A score-based model that denoises object positions, orientations and sizes inside any floor plan. One model handles generation, completion and re-arrangement.
